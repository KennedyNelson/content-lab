## OWNER'S CLEAN-AND-REAL RULES (owner directive 2026-11-09, after he rejected two cuts of the Sharma ji short; these beat any urge to add spice, effects or spectacle)
The owner's words: no exaggeration, no unnecessary objects on screen, no unnecessary or repeated dialogue, make it realistic, understand the humour of the video and keep it clean. Apply to every YouTube video:
- **Understated acting and events.** The joke lives in a realistic situation and small reactions (a deadpan 'Accha', a tired look). Banned unless the idea truly needs them: dramatic entrances (window crashes, slow-motion), crying droplets, sparkles or glints, spotlights, drip bags, trophies, thrown objects (a chappal), shaking cameras, big shrieks and wails. A 'dark' or 'spicy' brief means dry and awkward-true, NEVER louder or more violent: the first dark cut was rejected as 'a lot of exaggerations'.
- **Every object on screen must earn its place.** If the story still works without a prop or effect, delete it. Dark circles under the eyes are enough to show someone has not slept; do not add a gadget to explain it.
- **Every dialogue line must earn its place.** Never say the same thing twice (a word repeated in two lines, a character explaining what was already shown or said). Before building, read the script line by line and cut any line that repeats or only adds an exaggeration (cut: 'valuation 1000 crore', 'par neend nahi aati' after 'last slept in 2019'). Use simple, natural wording ('ek bada startup', not an over-specific claim).
- **Keep the story visually CLEAR.** Show cause and effect on screen: who is calling whom, the phone ringing in a hand with the caller name, the character looking at it, answering, holding it to the ear, then listening; the person who is speaking is visible; a family member is drawn as a FULL character (never only a hand or an off-screen voice) unless the owner asks otherwise. Nothing left for the viewer to guess.
- **Voice SPEED (owner directive 2026-11-09: Kalu's lines in the Sharma ji short were 'very slow', fix from now on).** Chill is carried by TONE, never by slowness. Every character, Kalu included, speaks at natural conversational speed: never put 'slow drawl' or 'slowly' in tags, never slow a clip with atempo below 1.0 (use 1.0-1.1), and keep pauses tight with no dead air. Kalu's lines are short, flat and low-energy but delivered at normal pace. In QA compare each line's duration with its word count (a chill line is about 2.5-3.5 syllables per second); if it is far slower, retake it or speed it up. **Owner-approved fast mix (2026-11-10, tested on the Sharma ji script: 23.95 s became 20.27 s and the owner approved it):** tags without 'slow'/'drawl' ([relaxed, unbothered, deadpan, casual]; add 'brisk' on the last line), `atempo` 1.1 for Kalu and 1.15 for the other characters (about 1.12 for the phone voice), per-line silence CAP 0.10-0.20 s, gaps before lines of 0.1-0.45 s (one-word reactions 0.15 s), and only the phone-ring beat gets a longer gap (about 1.8 s). Use these as the DEFAULT mix settings for every YouTube short; the old slower mix (atempo .95-1.1, gaps .35-.9 s) is rejected.
- **Voices carry the vibe.** A chill character (Kalu) must sound chill through tone: a stack like [relaxed, unbothered, deadpan, calm] with short flat sentences. Nobody shouts unless the owner asks; angry parent = [scolding, exasperated], phone parent = [worried, gentle but firm].
- **Duplicate-word check (required).** The v4 models sometimes say a word twice in one clip ('Bavan... Bavan', 'Accha... Accha'). After every generation run `ffmpeg -i clip.mp3 -af silencedetect=n=-35dB:d=0.12 -f null -` and count speech bursts: a one-word line must have ONE burst; trim the clip with `-t` at the end of the first burst, or retake. Never trust the script text alone.
- **Review loop.** When the owner is reviewing, send the video AND the individual voice-line clips (SendUserFile) before publishing, say plainly that Claude cannot hear them, and publish only after his OK. Expect several rounds; apply every note literally, then re-check the contact sheet and the line timings.

## CONTENT MIX (owner directive, 2026-11-07: 'keep it mixed so that it is fun and always surprising'; replaces any 'teach in every video' rule)
Each daily video is ONE of these four types. Claude picks the type from research, what is trending now, and the last YouTube videos logged in `studio/PUBLISHED.md`:
1. **Funny knowledge share.** Explain one intriguing, TRUE thing in a clean, simple, funny way (the Mission and the Stickman explainer format). Write ONE takeaway sentence first so the viewer can say what they learned; one main idea, simple words, one clear picture per beat, no stacked facts; fact-check against reliable sources and list them in `script.json`. The joke carries the fact, never replaces it.
2. **Extremely relatable comedy.** Something boys do, girls do, couples do, friends, family, office, exams, phones, AI chats: the viewer thinks 'this is literally me/us' and sends it to someone. No fact needed. Built as a short single-beat or short-dialogue gag: the relatable situation in the first frame (a short setup line), a quick setup, an overacted visual payoff, an abrupt or looping end. Use the single-beat Viral Reel styles and the reference styles in `shorts-animation-styles` for the look; the tone stays YouTube-friendly (see HUMOUR RULES). The scripts, dialogue and ideas are YouTube's own, written for this channel's audience.
3. **Dark or spicy comedy.** Dark humour, roast, awkward-truth and embarrassment jokes, a savage twist. On YouTube it stays advertiser-friendly and inside Claude's limits: no hate, no demeaning jokes about groups, no explicit content, no sexual innuendo or slurs; the edge sits in the situation and the twist.
4. **Emotional / cinematic.** A heart-touching, motivating or bittersweet short: a quiet cinematic look, big acting, an uplifting music cue that lands on the turn, a hopeful ending. It needs a feeling, not a fact.
Rules of the mix: do not post the same type two days in a row; log the type in `studio/PUBLISHED.md` with every video; keep funny knowledge videos in regular rotation (roughly one in every two or three) because teaching is still part of the brand, and let relatable, dark and emotional videos fill the rest, so it stays surprising. Hook, NEVER BORING, humour and QA rules apply to ALL four types; only the knowledge checks (takeaway sentence, true facts, sources) apply to type 1. When the owner asks for a specific type or idea, that wins.

## PACE AND ENGAGEMENT: CLAUDE DECIDES (owner directive of 2026-11-06, replaces every fixed numeric pacing limit)
The owner does NOT want hard limits on pace. For every video Claude decides, from research and judgment: how quickly the frames change, when they change, how fast the dialogue is, which objects and gags appear, how long the video is, and how much information it carries. The only target is a video that is ENGAGING and very interesting from the first frame to the last, light enough to watch for entertainment, and funny. Numbers elsewhere in this skill (for example '4.6 syllables per second', 'a shot every 1-2 s', word counts) are only reference points from past videos, never rules: break them whenever the story needs it, and say why in the log.

**What the owner has told us so far about YouTube videos (real feedback, treat as evidence):**
- Tadpole hiccups and doors-delete-memory (about 20 scenes in 36-40 s, 123-136 words, many facts, on-screen counters): too much information delivered too fast for the brain to process; topics not exciting enough; not funny enough.
- 382-days Neon Pit (slow voice, one locked scene all video): 'very slow and boring'. The voice was too slow and the picture never changed.
- The owner approved the speed and the constantly changing frames of the first two videos and wants them back, with less information per second and more humour and spice. The rebuilt fast Stickman 382-days video (24 scenes in 25 s, voice about 4.6 syllables per second, `studio/src/stickman-gary/ep-382.js`) is the current reference for 'right' on YouTube explainers.
- The lesson is not 'slow' or 'fast': it is CLEAR, FUNNY AND ALWAYS CHANGING (or acting). Keep the viewer's eyes busy with simple jokes, never with dense facts, and never leave a picture sitting still and lifeless. For single-beat relatable gags the engagement can come from an instantly relatable setup line, expressive acting and a visual payoff rather than rapid cuts; fast changing frames fit explainers.

**Research findings (practitioner heuristics from 2026-11-06, low confidence; verify each run):** the first ~3 seconds decide most swipes, so open on the most extreme true fact or a surprising picture; something new on screen every ~1.5-4 s depending on the joke; about 15-35 s is the sweet spot (comedy 18-28 s, quick facts 13-20 s, educational up to ~40 s) and the first draft gets cut hard; percent viewed matters more than length and loops lift replays; most viewers start with sound off, so burn in word-timed captions of 2-4 words.

**How to decide for each video (the PACING BRIEF, written before building, saved in the episode folder and summarised in PUBLISHED.md):**
1. Research first, every run: search YouTube Shorts in our niche (animated, faceless, stickman, funny facts, 'what if', relatable comedy) for what is viral NOW. Tool: the Composio connection, toolkit `scrapecreators` (YouTube search with sort_by popular and content_type shorts; YouTube transcripts). It returns views and likes, and the transcripts let you measure words per second and the scene rhythm of the winners. NOTE: on 2026-11-06 the Composio wallet was out of credits (calls cost about $0.001 each). If a call fails for credits, fall back to WebSearch and WebFetch and tell the owner once in the report so they can top up at the Composio dashboard. Also look at our own YouTube numbers (YouTube stats through Zapier raw GET) for which of our videos held viewers best.
2. From that evidence, write the brief: the idea and the one thing the viewer should remember; the hook (first second, picture and line); the intended rhythm of picture changes (where it speeds up, where it holds a beat for a joke or reveal, and why); the voice delivery and speed (Claude decides, and may vary it inside one video: quick for build-up, a beat of air before a punchline); the visual objects, props and gags that carry the humour; the length and why; how the ending loops or cuts off.
3. Build to the brief, look at the contact sheet, and judge as a viewer: 'would I stay, would I laugh, would I send this to a friend?' If a stretch feels slow, static or confusing, change it, whatever the brief said.
4. Log the decisions and the results: after posting, note the pace choices in `studio/PUBLISHED.md`; on later runs check the views and average watch time of recent videos and let those results shape the next brief. Add new lessons to this skill when the owner reacts.

**Principles that stay (they are about clarity, not speed):**
- Never leave one picture sitting through the whole video unless a face or body is acting and the setup line is strong; otherwise always something moving, changing or reacting. A single locked scene with nothing happening was rejected.
- Do not make the brain work: simple sentences, one clear picture per beat, one main idea, few facts, nothing that needs rewinding, every joke understandable on first listen. If a viewer would need to focus hard, simplify.
- Keep the voice lively and natural; never drag it out. Emotion lives in pitch and loudness, and silences are tight except where a beat of air makes a punchline land.
- No reading while listening: no counters, banners, signs or labels in the picture; captions only (owner's rule). The one exception is the top SETUP LINE of the viral reel styles.
- Bright, friendly visuals; energy from humour and surprise (look-and-feel rules in `shorts-animation-styles`).
- Before publishing ask: 'Is this engaging from the first second to the last, and funny?' If not, rework it.

## NEVER BORING (quality bar, hard rule)
No simple, basic or flat videos. Every video must be very creative, humorous and awe-inspiring so viewers stay to the end to find out what happened (the way 'WHAT IF' videos do). Boring topics are as bad as boring execution: the owner said the topics were still not exciting and intriguing, and wants a LOT more spice in the topic and in the way it is presented. Before building, check the script against this list and rewrite until it passes:
- **Hook in the first second with the most extreme specific fact or image.** The strongest hooks are a concrete, absurd, TRUE headline, stated flat and fast, not a generic question or a mild claim. Owner's example: for the hiccup video the hook should have been 'A farmer had hiccups for 68 years.' (Charles Osborne, 1922-1990), NOT 'There is a tadpole in your throat' (the farmer fact was buried at line 8 of 13). Pull the single most jaw-dropping real record, person, number or consequence out of the topic and open with it, then explain the why afterwards. Other shapes that work: a person already in trouble ('A man lost his memory every time he walked through a door'), an impossible 'what if', a scary specific number. No intros, no greetings, no 'today we will', no 'did you know' with a mild fact, no vague claim like 'Doors are deleting your memory. And it gets worse.'
- **Topic spice test (do it in research, before writing).** Reject a topic unless it has at least one of: a real jaw-dropping record or extreme case; a real person with a wild story; a gross, scary, forbidden-feeling or 'wait, that is actually true?' angle; a direct hit on something the viewer does daily and has never questioned. Then spice the framing: give the idea a villain, a mystery, a rivalry, a ticking clock or a bet. Prefer 'The man who...', 'The day...', 'Why your body secretly...' over textbook phrasing ('A Tadpole Is Causing Your Hiccups' and 'Why Doors Delete Your Memory' were too mild). Write 5 candidate hooks per topic and keep the most extreme TRUE one. (The extreme-true-fact part is for knowledge videos; the other types need an instantly relatable, darkly funny or moving idea.)
- **Open a curiosity gap** in the first 3 seconds and do not close it until the last 3 seconds. Tease the payoff, but always deliver.
- **Escalate:** each beat is bigger, weirder or funnier than the one before (a ladder: 1 day, 1 week, 1 year...), at most three steps. Never a flat list of facts.
- **Laughs over facts.** A genuine laugh beat every few seconds, built from character, exaggeration and surprise, never from jargon. At least one WOW fact or image and one unexpected twist, and the twist must be funny. Do not stack facts between jokes.
- **Relatable moment:** at least one line or gag the viewer thinks 'that is literally me'.
- **Payoff + lesson + loop:** a satisfying ending that teaches or comforts, and a last line or image that loops back to the first (so it replays).
- **Original and unusual angle:** if a viewer could have seen the same idea on a hundred channels, change the angle, the scenario or the character until it feels new.

## HUMOUR RULES (owner: 'be a lot more funny and humorous')
- In comedy videos humour is the main product; in knowledge videos humour and the one clear thing taught are both the product. Aim for at least 5-6 real laugh beats in a 35 s comedy or knowledge short, one of them in the first 5 s (an emotional short needs a feeling that lands instead).
- **Natural, not written (owner feedback on Claude's first scripts: 'not that funny, unnatural, not viral-worthy').** Jokes must sound like what a real 26-year-old Indian says to a friend, not like a clever written line. No forced wordplay, no corporate or 'stretch goal' jargon, no tidy punchline structure. Read each line aloud as a friend would say it; if it sounds written, rewrite it or drop it. Real, specific, observed details beat invented cleverness.
- Funny tools to use every video: absurd comparisons ('your brain has a tiny intern named Gary who bins your thoughts'), a recurring running gag or sidekick, exaggerated reactions, deadpan understatement after something huge, callbacks (a joke from the first 5 s returns at the end), a ridiculous precise number, a character who confidently explains it wrong first.
- Relatable everyday-India and universal humour is welcome (family, exams, phone battery, traffic, office life). **YouTube Shorts: keep it kind and advertiser-friendly**: dark-ish and cheeky is fine, but no sexual innuendo, no slurs or swearing, no stereotypes, and no jokes against a race, religion, caste or community. Never racist or hateful, never explicit.
- In a funny knowledge video, write the one-sentence takeaway first, then build the best joke around it so the joke carries the fact instead of replacing it. In relatable, dark and emotional videos, write the joke or the feeling first. If a line is information only, make it shorter or fold it into a joke, but never delete the fact a knowledge video exists to teach.
- Short punchy joke wording; no filler; say punchlines clean and together.

Also search the web, especially YouTube Shorts and what is trending and shared right now (AI and Claude humour, science, space, the human body, history, survival, 'what if' questions, unnoticed everyday things, relatable boy/girl/couple behaviour). See which kinds of videos in the niche get huge views, take the good bits, then create our OWN idea. First pick today's CONTENT MIX type. Then apply the TOPIC SPICE TEST: pick the single topic with the strongest viral potential, relatability and a jaw-dropping extreme fact (knowledge) or an instantly relatable, darkly funny or moving idea (other types). Clone the repo and read `studio/PUBLISHED.md`: never repeat an idea or script. The same FORMAT may return (another chat short) but with a completely new scenario, jokes and look.
2. **Choose the look.** If a person is needed, it is Kalu in Desi Goggle-Eye Toon (see CHARACTERS); otherwise pick from the STYLE PICKER in `shorts-animation-styles` (match the idea, and do not repeat either of the last two YouTube looks in `studio/PUBLISHED.md`). For built-in faceless-shorts styles load the `faceless-shorts` skill. Skip any intake or 'Build it?' steps: use defaults.
3. **Script.** Length and word count come from the pacing brief (past videos for reference only: explainers about 55-75 words in 25-35 s; chat and banter 7-10 short lines in 15-25 s). Extreme hook first (a jaw-dropping true fact for knowledge videos; a relatable setup line or a striking image for the other types), ONE main idea, a funny twist, a last line that loops back to the start, funniest beat early. Original wording only. Write the PACING BRIEF first, then run the NEVER BORING and HUMOUR checklists and rewrite until they pass. Fact-check against reliable sources and list them in `script.json`. Title: short, curious, under 60 characters, built around the extreme fact or person where possible. Fix every line, tone and emotion tag now.
4. **Vary the setting** every video: set, props, side characters and their outfits, music. Kalu himself never changes. Log it.
5. **Voiceover** with ElevenLabs model `eleven_v4` (Starter plan, 30,234 credits a month) using the toolkit in `shorts-animation-styles`: check the live balance first, write each line like a director, retake flat lines. The YouTube description needs 'Voices: ElevenLabs'.

7. **QA (required).** Render a contact sheet and LOOK at the frames. Check which CONTENT MIX type it is and that it delivers it (knowledge: the viewer can say in one sentence what they learned and every fact is true; relatable: instantly recognisable and sendable; dark: funny and inside the limits; emotional: the feeling lands), and check length, captions match audio, lip-sync is on the right speaker, no glitches, audio not silent, nothing offensive, facts correct, and that it matches its pacing brief and passes the PACE AND ENGAGEMENT principles and the NEVER BORING list (never static, easy to follow, funny, no on-screen counters or labels, hook is the most extreme true fact, engaging to the last frame). Fix and re-render. If it still fails, upload PRIVATE and report; never publish public.
8. **Host.** Re-encode (`ffmpeg -nostdin -i out.mp4 -c:v libx264 -crf 17 -preset slow -maxrate 12M -bufsize 24M -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -movflags +faststart YYYY-MM-DD-slug.mp4`), copy to the repo root, commit and push to main. 9. **Publish.** Zapier YouTube `upload_video` with video = `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KennedyNelson/funwithken-videos/main/<file>.mp4`: privacy public, the title unchanged, description of one or two catchy sentences plus 3-5 hashtags (include #Shorts, and 'Voices: ElevenLabs' whenever ElevenLabs voices are used), 5 tags, the best category (knowledge videos: 28 Science & Technology or 27 Education; relatable, dark and emotional videos: 23 Comedy or 24 Entertainment; never the default), made_for_kids false, language en. More than one video a day is fine: if something was already posted today, still publish public. Every run publishes public, scheduled or manual; the schedule fires once a day and any extra run started by hand also goes public. PRIVATE is only for a video that fails QA. Permanently deleting a video is not allowed for Claude: set it private and tell the owner to delete it in YouTube Studio.
10. **Log.** Append to `studio/PUBLISHED.md` (date, channel YouTube, content type, idea, style, title, link, look, voices, credits used, pace choices; and chat looks in `studio/LOOKLOG.md`) and push.
11. **Report** to the owner: title, link, ElevenLabs credits used and left, anything fixed during QA. On failure say which step and why.
