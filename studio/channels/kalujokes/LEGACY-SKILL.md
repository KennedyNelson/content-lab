# Instagram Reels studio (account kaalujokes): Instagram strategy and process

This skill is for Instagram ONLY and is completely separate from the YouTube skill `fun-with-ken-studio` (owner directive, 2026-11-07). It does not follow the YouTube logic: no daily schedule, no knowledge or informational content, no advertiser-friendly tone, no YouTube pacing rules or content mix. Instagram reels are relatable, spicy comedy for a young, modern Indian audience; topics, scripts and dialogue are different from YouTube. Informational content does not work here.

The ONLY thing shared with YouTube is the look-and-build toolkit in the skill **`shorts-animation-styles`**: the same exact animation styles (Stickman, the four VIRAL REEL STYLES, the ten reference styles, Neon Pit, built-in and Lemo styles), the style picker, shot vocabulary, VARY EVERY VIDEO, CHARACTER CREATIVITY, look-and-feel rules, the audio-driven build, BGM presets (`studio/bgm.py`), render commands, and the ElevenLabs toolkit (balance check, voice IDs, emotion tags, Devanagari pronunciation, the 402 limit). Load `shorts-animation-styles` whenever a reel is built, and do not copy any YouTube channel rules into a reel. Repo: `KennedyNelson/funwithken-videos` (local clone `/home/claude/funwithken-videos`). Spicy and relatable reels made here may also be reused on YouTube Shorts (milder cut), but never the other way round.

## 1. The owner supplies the idea (owner directive, 2026-11-06)
Claude's own invented reel ideas and script were judged 'not that funny, unnatural and not viral-worthy'. So:
- Wait for the owner's idea. Keep the owner's joke and wording. Claude does the work around it: natural spoken dialogue in the owner's language (section 2A), timing, scene and gag design, characters, voices, build, QA, caption, posting.
- Claude may suggest small tweaks (a visual gag, a sharper last beat) but does not invent the topic or jokes unless the owner asks for ideas.
- If asked for ideas, base them on the idea bank and the research below (real viral reels), not on invented 'AI-sounding' jokes, and let the owner pick.
- In unattended runs with no owner idea, do not post an invented reel.

## 2. Audience, tone and limits
**Owner directive (2026-11-06): Instagram reels must be very spicy, funny, relatable and humorous, with a viral factor and a hook, for a young and modern audience.**
- **Go bold on:** dark humour and dark comedy, savage roasts between friends, self-roast, cheeky and suggestive innuendo, street slang and swearing in the reel's language (Hindi/Hinglish or English) used the way young friends really talk (casual abuses like 'saala', 'bhenchod' or 'chutiya' as part of natural dialogue, not as an attack on a real person), taboo-but-relatable truths (money, breakups, mental health, family, flirting, exams, jobs, gym, couples, dads, friends). Sometimes cute, emotional or feel-good. Relatability is the main attractor.
- **Body and looks humour (widened, owner directive 2026-11-07):** go hard on our own fictional characters' bodies and looks. Fat jokes, belly, double chin, skinny, short, tall, dark ('kala / lamba'), bad hair, ugly-face roasts are all allowed and can be savage, as long as the target is one of our fictional characters inside the story (Kalu, his friend, the girlfriend, the dad) and not real people or a whole group (no 'all fat people are...' jokes).
- **Claude's own limits, applied silently and not negotiable (they stay even though our characters are AI-generated, because a joke built on a group still targets the real group):** no racist jokes and no jokes that attack or demean a race, religion, caste, community, nationality, gender or disability; no body-shaming of real people or of groups (roasting our own characters' bodies is allowed, see above); no explicit sexual content (innuendo is fine). When the owner's idea needs one of these, Claude keeps the dark or spicy energy but moves the punchline to a safer target (the character, the situation, the relationship, the speaker himself) and says so in one line, offering the alternative. Beyond principle, hate-based jokes and slurs also get reels removed or demoted and can cost the account its reach.
- Check the platform risk silently: heavy swearing or sexual innuendo in the first seconds can limit reach. Spice sits in the punchline and the attitude, and the hook should work even if audio is muted.
- Hindi/Hinglish is welcome in lines and captions when the idea is in Hinglish (section 2A).
- Jokes must sound natural, like what a real 26-year-old Indian says to a friend, not like a clever written line (owner feedback: 'not that funny, unnatural, not viral-worthy'). Read each line aloud as a friend would say it.

## 2B. Dynamic outfits for Kalu and Battery (owner directive, 2026-11-15)
For EVERY reel, choose and create each character's outfit creatively and dynamically (never reuse the same outfit by default): any t-shirt colour and any pant colour; t-shirt type can be oversized, regular fit or sleeveless; bottoms can be shorts, trackpants, jeans or denim. Applies to Kalu, Battery and any other character. Faces, hair, skin tone and the flat 2D style stay exactly as on the final v26 character sheet; only the clothes change. Pick colours that suit the skin tone, the scene and the joke, make Kalu and Battery clearly different from each other, and tell the owner the chosen outfit in one line. The outfit stays the same across all shots of one reel. Stills are changed with a Flow edit of an existing character still ('change only the outfit').

## 2C. Frame and mouth rules for dialogue reels (owner directive, 2026-11-15)
- Two characters talking: keep BOTH visible in one locked two-shot at all times; no cutting to single close-ups. Change only poses/expressions through still variants swapped at the right word (for example a shock face the instant the trigger word is said).
- Open mouths: smaller and placed lower on the face, never touching the nose; open-mouth style stays as on the sheet (black outline, maroon inside, white teeth on top, pink tongue).
- Line delivery: casual lines (e.g. accepting tea) are said normally, not exaggerated; keep emotion for the lines that need it.

## 4. How a viral relatable reel is made (formula)
1. Start with a feeling or situation the viewer has lived ('me when', 'POV', 'dad things', 'friend at the gym', 'when she pays the bill'), never a fact or a lesson.
2. Put the setup line on screen in the first frame, written like a friend's caption, in the reel's language (section 2A). The viewer should get the whole joke in one second.
3. Short setup, then a reveal, escalation or overacted reaction within 2-4 s, then the payoff, then stop abruptly or loop. Length is decided by the joke: 5-8 s single beat, 12-20 s dialogue or two-step escalation.
4. Make it sendable: ask 'who would I send this to?' and end with a tag/mention/comment hook ('tag that friend', 'drop your number') only where it is natural.
5. Acting does the work: big readable eyes, slumps, jaw drops, deadpan stares. Characters live in a real set with depth. Cute and emotional versions use big-eyes characters and a warm feel.
6. Audio: natural conversational speed dialogue in the reel's language, tight silences, light BGM, and high-emotion voices (see section 10); a trending sound cannot be attached through the API, so write the joke so it works with our voice and music or without audio.
7. Caption: short, 2-5 words, then exactly 5 relevant hashtags (owner rule). Location Indore, M.P. Post as a REEL (9:16).
8. Look: choose from the styles in `shorts-animation-styles` (the four VIRAL REEL STYLES: Moody Mono, Pastel Call, Storybook Room, Big-Eyes Cutie; Stickman; or one of the 10 reference styles; pick the one that fits the joke, and vary it from reel to reel); bright unless a moody emotional piece needs dark; the establishing and payoff shots are two-shots with both characters in frame; no on-screen text beyond captions and the top setup line; voices plus BGM only; abrupt endings. Never a single locked frame for the whole reel: see section 12 (dynamic shots) and section 11 (owner's production preferences).

## 7. Step-by-step process for each reel
1. Get the owner's idea (section 1). If none, offer 3 pitches taken from the idea bank (section 9) and let the owner choose.
2. Write the setup line and the punchline in ONE sentence each and run the checklist in section 8 BEFORE building anything. Write two versions of the setup line and pick the stronger.
3. Write the lines in natural speech in the owner's language (Hinglish only when the idea is in Hinglish, section 2A), keeping the owner's wording, and mark the emotion of every line (section 10).
4. Choose the look (section 4 point 8) and keep the same two characters and setting across a series where the owner wants one. Write the SHOT LIST (section 12) with a new shot at least every 1.5-3 s and at least one visual gag, and apply the owner's production preferences (section 11).
5. Voices (section 10 and the ElevenLabs toolkit in `shorts-animation-styles`), build (Stickman engine and render commands in `shorts-animation-styles`), contact sheet, QA, and LISTEN-check the emotion of each line if audio can be checked (see section 10).
6. Send the render to the owner and wait for the OK or corrections (section 11 delivery workflow). Then caption: 2-5 words plus exactly 5 hashtags, location Indore, M.P., post as a Reel through Composio, public.
7. Log in `studio/PUBLISHED.md` (idea, setup line, hook type, look, length, series or not).
8. After 24-48 hours read the insights (section 6) and add the numbers to the same log line.

## 8. Before building and before posting
- Is it a feeling the viewer has lived? Is the joke clear in one second? Is it the owner's joke in natural words? Is there a visual punchline within about 2-4 s?
- WHO would send it, and to whom ('send this to your gym friend / dad / roommate')? A clear send-to person is the target. Mention it in the caption only if it feels natural.
- Does it have a simple comment hook where natural ('drop your number', 'tag that friend')?
- Is it spicy enough for this audience, and does every spicy beat stay inside Claude's limits (section 2)?
- If it is not funny when read aloud, drop it and ask for another idea instead of polishing it.
- Plus the checklist of section 13.4.

## 9. Learning loop and idea bank
- **Insights log:** keep one table in `studio/PUBLISHED.md` with views, reach, average watch time, shares, saves, setup-line type, theme and length for every reel. Every 10 reels write a short learning note (what held viewers, what did not) into this skill.
- **Change one thing per reel** (hook style, length or theme) so the results are readable.
- **Series:** reuse the same two characters and place so repeat viewers can follow.
- **Idea bank:** keep a running list `studio/IDEA_BANK.md` of relatable situations seen in real viral reels (couples, dads, gym, best friends, money and bills, exams and relatives, 'wait for the end' reveals) with the reference reel noted. Pitch from it, never invent AI-sounding jokes.

## 10. Voices with strong, clear emotion (owner directive, 2026-11-06: 'high emotions and variations: happiness, anger, upset, all clearly in the voice; make sure ElevenLabs does the job properly')
The tooling (eleven_v3, tags, stability, voice IDs, credits, profanity, Devanagari, the free-plan limit) is in the ElevenLabs toolkit of `shorts-animation-styles`. The Instagram rules on top:
- **It must sound like a real human, never like AI (owner directive, 2026-11-07).** Anger, happiness, being upset, high energy, sarcasm and panic must be clearly audible, the way a real person sounds in a real moment. Concretely:
  - Write lines the way people actually speak, not like written sentences: fillers and breaks ('arre', 'yaar', 'bro', 'wait wait wait', 'I mean...'), half-finished words, repeated words when excited or angry ('No no no NO'), interruptions.
  - Use the human non-verbal tags of eleven_v3 where they fit: [laughs], [chuckles], [sighs], [scoffs], [gasps], [groans], [sniffs], [crying], [shouting], [whispers], [excited], [frustrated], [sarcastic], [nervous]. One or two per line, placed where a real person would do it.
  - Keep stability low (the most expressive setting the toolkit allows) for emotional lines so pitch and pace actually move; never a flat, evenly paced read.
  - Real people change pace inside a line: speed up when excited or panicking, slow down and drag words when upset or sarcastic, punch single words when angry. Do not normalise every line to the same speed or loudness.
  - After generating, check for robotic tells: even pacing, every sentence ending on the same pitch, emotion tags read out loud, wrong stress. A line with any of these is flagged to the owner and regenerated if credits allow.
- **Marked emotion on every line.** Before voicing, write the emotion and intensity next to each line in the script (happy, excited, angry, shouting, upset, crying, scared, sarcastic, deadpan, smug, embarrassed, whispering, exhausted, laughing). A reel should swing between at least two or three clearly different emotions or intensities (for example calm setup, then rising irritation, then a blown-up shout or a cracked sob), because contrast is what makes the voice feel alive.
- Put tags before the words they colour, for example `[angry, shouting] Saale, tune phir se mera charger le liya!`; use capitals, '!', '?', '...' and short sentences for anger. When the reel is in Hinglish, it is written the way it is spoken, with the key Hindi words in Devanagari inside the text sent to the voice and Latin only on screen.
- **Voice variation:** a different voice per character with clearly different pitch and age; never the same voice for both; match the voice to the character (a gruff voice for the angry dad, a lighter voice for the nervous friend). Vary voices between reels.
- **Post-processing:** keep the emotion in pitch and loudness. Do not over-compress shouts: mild `atempo` only, tighten silences instead, duck the BGM under shouts. No sound effects unless the owner asks.
- **Credits:** the emotion tags add characters, so reels must be short and lines tight, and each line is generated ONCE with no retakes. If a line comes back flat, regenerate it only when credits allow and tell the owner. If the free credits cannot cover the reel, say so and ask whether to top up the plan or to use the free local voice; never hide a weaker voice.
- **Check before posting:** if you can listen to the audio, confirm every line sounds like the marked emotion. If you cannot listen (the browser cannot play audio), say so in the report and ask the owner to listen to the first render and name the lines that feel flat; fix only those lines.

### 13.1 The 10 reference animation styles
The ten looks (painted dusk street, flat bright desi cartoon, big-head deadpan doodle on photo backgrounds, macro-insert gag, phone-screen insert, anime-detailed travelling-camera location, chibi cute couple, cel-shaded night peek, warm lit room with a window, object-as-character gag) are now defined in the shared skill `shorts-animation-styles` under REFERENCE STYLES, with their use-for notes and the style picker. ### 13.2 Shared craft in all 14
- **ONE short first-frame line** that frames the situation, written like a caption: 'Pov: ...', '... for no reason', 'Guys after 7 PM', '... be like', 'X ne Y dekh liya', 'Me at X'. In English or Hinglish, big bold text in a rounded box, top third of the frame. Our sarcastic top line already fits this: keep it, and prefer these patterns.
- **Situation first, plot second.** The viewer recognises their own life in under a second (mom checking on you, dad catching a status, phone dying, a wedding you did not want to attend, a stranger's splash on the road). The reel only shows what then happens.
- **One escalation, one payoff, then stop.** No second plot. A visual reversal or an overacted reaction closes it. Several reels freeze on the shocked face or end mid-action. Keep abrupt endings.
- **Camera never stays still:** every reel mixes wide, mid, reaction and an extreme close-up or insert. The most viral ones (macro gags, car-splash, scooter) change angle on almost every beat. This confirms section 12.
- **Acting size:** huge eyes, tiny pupils, open jaws, pop-eyes, sweat and tear marks, slow push-in on a frozen face. The face is the punchline; the line only points at it.
- **Characters:** ONE consistent look per creator so viewers recognise the account. Kalu is the café-reel Kalu (KALU CANON in shorts-animation-styles) in every reel, no redesigns unless the owner asks. Battery is his recurring friend.
- **Sends beat likes.** Shares were often near or above comments by 10-300x (3.5M shares vs 10.9K comments). Every idea must pass: 'which specific person would someone send this to, with what message?' (mom, dad, roommate, gym friend, ex, single friend). Design the last beat so that sending it says something ('this is you', 'this is us').
- **Topic bank that repeated:** mom and dad habits, WhatsApp and phone moments, texting a parent, weddings and functions, trains and travel embarrassment, couples and fake-account tests, being single, school/kid vs adult contrast, money and delivery, small humiliating accidents. Roughly half were desi-family situations.
- **Audio:** about a third used trending Bollywood or Punjabi tracks (we cannot attach those through the API); the rest used voice or sound cues. Our fallback is voices plus a rotating BGM preset and abrupt ending.
- **Caption:** often just an emoji, a 2-5 word Hinglish line, or a short joke; hashtags vary. Our rule (2-5 words plus EXACTLY 5 hashtags) stays.

### 13.3 Humour and spice: what worked
- **Embarrassment and exposure humour** (a parent seeing a status, a private message going wrong, an accident in public) is the most shared kind, and it stays inside our limits because the target is the character in an awkward situation.
- **Dark or cheeky edge sits in the situation or the last beat**, not in slurs or attacks on a group. Innuendo and single-life roasts ('25 yrs old single me') are fine.
- **Self-roast and relationship roast** ('Pizza karwao', 'Mom's phone brightness') beat generic jokes.
- **Contrast is funny:** innocent setup vs savage payoff, calm face vs scream, cute couple vs dark twist, 14-year-old vs 25-year-old energy.
- Never copy a reel's shots, characters, brand look or audio one for one: use the style and the lesson, with our own characters, the owner's joke and our own build.

2. Is there a macro insert, phone insert or object cutaway at least once?
3. Is there ONE escalation and ONE visual payoff, then a hard stop?
4. Which of the 10 reference styles is this, and is it different from the previous reel?
5. Who is the 'send it to' person, and does the final beat make the sender look good for sending it?
6. Is the main face acted at a big size (eyes, mouth) in the payoff shot?
7. Is the edge in the situation or the target a fictional character, not a group of people?
