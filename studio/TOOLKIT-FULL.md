# Shorts animation styles and build toolkit (shared by YouTube and Instagram)

This skill holds HOW a video looks and how it is built. It holds NO channel strategy. Topics, scripts, dialogue, tone, humour limits, pacing decisions, posting and analytics live in the two channel skills, which are completely separate:
- `fun-with-ken-studio`: YouTube Shorts (channel FunWithKen). Knowledge sharing plus relatable, humorous, funny; advertiser-friendly.
- `instagram-reels-studio`: Instagram Reels (account kaalujokes). Relatable, spicy, unrestricted-within-limits comedy from the OWNER's ideas.
Both channels use exactly the same styles from this skill. Load this skill together with the channel skill whenever a video is built. Never import one channel's strategy into the other because both use this toolkit.

## Repo, engine and where things live
Code, engine and logs live in the GitHub repo **KennedyNelson/funwithken-videos** (folder `studio/`: `src`, `tools`, `vendor`, `fonts`, `refs`, `variants`, `episodes`, `bgm.py`, `PUBLISHED.md`, `LOOKLOG.md`). Add it with add_repo (push access) and clone it at the start of a run (local clone from earlier sessions: `/home/claude/funwithken-videos`, branch main). Videos are hosted as MP4s in the repo and used through raw GitHub URLs. Commits end with the attribution lines the session gives. Each channel keeps its own entries in `studio/PUBLISHED.md` (name the channel on every line).

**Stickman engine** `studio/src/stickman-gary/engine.js`. Exports: W, H, INK, bind, setBoil, ease, back, lerp, clamp, line, shape, rect, oval, dot, text, star, drop, sticky, man, face, walk, caption, groupWords, finish, ring. An episode module exports `DUR`, `init(ctx, words)`, `draw(t)` and `cues`. Camera helper pattern: `cam(z,x,y,shake,t,fn,rot)` (translate to centre, scale, translate back, small sin/cos shake). Characters are drawn feet-up from wobbly polygons; face types: open, scream, smile, smirk, frown, flat, wobble, tongue, grit; eyelids supported.

**Render commands** (run from `studio/`):
- `node src/stickman-gary/render.mjs <epRootWithData> <epModuleName> frames <dir> "t1,t2"` (single frames)
- `... sheet out.jpg <step> <cols>` (contact sheet)
- `... video out.mp4 30` (full render; needs `data/mix.wav` and `data/words.json`; an episode that reads `data/meta.json` needs that too; about 2 minutes for 25 s)
- then a CRF 22 `veryfast` re-encode (about 20 MB) for sharing, or the final host encode `ffmpeg -nostdin -i out.mp4 -c:v libx264 -crf 17 -preset slow -maxrate 12M -bufsize 24M -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -movflags +faststart <file>.mp4`.
An episode folder `studio/episodes/<date>-<slug>/` holds `asm.py` (audio assembler), `lines.json`, `raw/*.mp3`, `data/`.

## Stylesheets

### CODE ANIMATION PACK (10 looks from the owner's 'Claude Animation Styles' zip, added 2026-11-07; for BOTH channels)
All ten are drawn entirely in code (canvas 2D, or three.js for the 3D ones): no stock footage, no image generators, no copied artwork. The full pack lives in the repo at `studio/styles-pack/`: `references/styles/<style>.md` (the rules of each look, with a code starter), `references/workflow.md` (script to beats to frames, continuity sheet), `references/checklist.md` (10-point pre-export check), `references/player-template.html` (one-file player with a deterministic `draw(t)`, seeded `rand()`, `ease`, `noise1`, scrub and an in-browser Export button) and `scripts/render.mjs` (frame-exact MP4). READ the chosen style file in full before building; its rules beat habits.

| Style (say) | File | The look in one line | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pixel Art (/pixelart) | `pixel-art.md` | real square pixels: draw at 180x320 and scale x6 with smoothing off, integer positions, 1-px outlines, 16-24 colour palette, Bayer dither | game-language life jokes, retro, tech |
| Chalkboard (/chalkboard) | `chalkboard.md` | green/slate board, multi-pass grainy chalk strokes that draw themselves, hatching, 1-2 accent chalks, eraser wipes | teacher explainers, diagrams, formulas, cause and effect |
| Glow Sketch (/glowsketch) | `glow-sketch.md` | outlines traced in small glowing dots on near-black, one gold accent on the key object | calm 'did you know', wonder, elegant explainers |
| PS2 (/ps2) | `ps2.md` | low-poly flat-shaded three.js rendered at quarter size and upscaled soft, ambient + one light, blob shadows, fog | 2000s nostalgia, cutscene-style stories |
| Comic Art (/comicart) | `comic-art.md` | heavy brush ink, flat print colours, Ben-Day halftone shading, cream paper, panels, sound words, speed lines | action, before/after, step comedy, big reactions |
| Flat Vector (/flatvector) | `flat-vector.md` | no outlines, 2-3 flat planes per object, 6-8 colour palette with one accent, masked reveals, parallax | product/app/business explainers, clean editorial |
| Line-and-Dot Minimal (/dankoe; inspired by Dan Koe's visuals) | `dan-koe.md` | pure black, thin white lines with dots at the joints, objects as constellations, lots of empty space | mindset, habits, money, focus ideas |
| CGI Motion (/cgimotion) | `cgi-motion.md` | cinematic three.js: physical materials, key/fill/rim light, ACES tone mapping, haze, tracking shots | epic moments: cars, sport, space, nature at scale |
| Toy Brick (/lego; inspired by brick films) | `lego.md` | everything built from glossy studded bricks on a real grid, stop-motion stepped on twos, snap-together assembly | builds, cities, machines, playful stories |
| Clay 3D Explainer (/zackd; inspired by ZackD Films) | `zackd-films.md` | soft rounded vinyl-clay three.js, bright soft light, cutaways and close-ups that demonstrate each claim | 'what happens if' science and body explainers |

**Pack house rules (keep them):** the owner's script is the story (keep words, order, facts); every beat SHOWS what is being said in the style's own drawing language (a palette swap is not a new style); one focal point at a time, readable on a phone, keep words out of the top 240 px and bottom 350 px in 9:16 and nothing important within 80 px of an edge; keep characters, props and colours consistent; no real brand logos, no copied characters or trademarked minifigures, no real people's faces (named looks are technique inspirations only, never copies; call them by our names in captions and logs, never the brand's); deterministic `draw(t)` (no `Math.random()` or clocks at draw time); edits change only what was asked; a beat list sits in a comment at the top of the file.

**How a pack style fits OUR pipeline (our rules win where they differ):**
1. Audio first, as always (AUDIO-DRIVEN BUILD): ElevenLabs voices or the free local voice, `asm.py` writes `data/mix.wav`, `data/words.json` and `data/meta.json`. The audio length sets the duration; beats follow the line timings.
2. Build from `studio/styles-pack/references/player-template.html`: set `CONFIG` (1080x1920, 30 fps, duration from the mix), load the words/meta JSON in `setup()` (or inline them), draw in `draw(t)`. 3D looks load three.js r128 from cdnjs as the template says.
3. Captions: our channels want word captions (Instagram also wants the one top setup line), so add them even though the pack defaults to none; style them to fit the look (chalk lettering, comic boxes, pixel font, thin pale sans) inside the safe area.
4. Render: `node studio/styles-pack/scripts/render.mjs <file>.html <out>.mp4 --audio <episode>/data/mix.wav` (`--preview` saves 5 PNG frames for QA). Node, ffmpeg and Playwright are in our sandbox. Then the usual host encode (H.264 yuv420p, faststart). Run `checklist.md` plus our contact-sheet QA before sending.
5. The owner's look-and-feel rules still apply: bright and readable unless the look is dark by design (Glow Sketch, Line-and-Dot, Chalkboard are dark-background styles; keep them clean and high-contrast, never murky), no extra on-screen info, voices plus light BGM only (no SFX unless asked), abrupt endings, dynamic shots per the channel skill, vary every video, log the style in `studio/PUBLISHED.md` and `studio/LOOKLOG.md`.
6. Status: none of the 10 pack looks has been rendered for either channel yet. Check the first one carefully (frames, timing, captions) before sending it to the owner.

### POLISHED DESI TOON STYLES (from the owner's last 5 saved reels on kaalujokes, watched frame by frame on 2026-11-07; for BOTH channels)
Reference reels (grammar only, never copy their characters, art, captions or audio): a dad-and-son bike ride reel (kp_animat...), a two-friends-on-a-bike reel over a real road, a 'father in his prime' home reel (dream_illustrations), a 'girls with their best friend vs me with my friend' scooter/bike reel, and a stick-body family reel (LaughByAnimation). All 13-20 s, 9:16, one main set, one simple label at the top.

**Shared craft of all five (this is the upgrade over our older looks):**
- **Clean, smooth vector lines.** Uniform dark outlines about 4-6 px at 1080 wide, smooth curves, NO hand-wobble or boil (turn the Stickman jitter off: amp 0). Line colour can be a dark brown/near-black.
- **Cel shading on everything.** Each surface has a base colour plus one darker shadow tone on the side away from the light (skin under the jaw, inside arms, under the belly; cloth folds and creases; hair underside) and a small light highlight (hair shine, glossy eyes, chrome). Clip the shadow shape to the base shape.
- **Real Indian proportions and costume.** Adults look like adults: dad with a belly, white vest (baniyan) and lungi or dhoti, chappals, moustache, specs, arm and leg hair strokes; young guys in tees, jeans, sneakers or flip-flops; women in kurtas and dupattas. Faces stay cartoon: big round white eyes with pupils and highlights, expressive brows, mouths that show teeth when angry or terrified.
- **Recognisable, detailed props and vehicles.** Bikes with spoked or alloy wheels, chrome, mirrors, mudguards, engine block, side stickers (invented words, never a real brand); scooters with curved body panels; furniture with patterns (floral chair upholstery), a newspaper with a Devanagari masthead, a floor lamp, curtains. Draw them from a mental reference of the real object, part by part. See the AUTO RICKSHAW SPEC below.
- **Backgrounds with depth.** Either a painted Indian street or house (tiled roof, plaster walls, trees, power lines) or a soft real-photo-like scene, rendered BLURRED like a shallow depth of field (blur about 12-20 px), with haze and a warm golden or bright daylight tint. The characters stay sharp in front, which sells the depth.
- **Speed and life.** Ride scenes: camera locked to the side of the vehicle, background scrolling fast with horizontal motion blur, vehicle bobbing and vibrating a few px, wheels spinning, hair and dupatta flapping. Rooms: slow push-ins, blinks, breathing, page turns. There is always micro-motion.
- **Light.** One clear light direction; rooms can have a dramatic diagonal window-light shape across the wall and floor and a lamp; outdoors use golden hour or bright day. Keep it bright and readable (OWNER'S LOOK-AND-FEEL RULES).
- **Top label.** One white rectangular box with black text in the top third (sans or a classic serif), one keyword in red, optional emoji. This replaces our white-and-yellow outlined top line when the look calls for it. Word captions at the bottom as usual.

**The five looks:**
1. **Desi Ride Toon.** Two characters on a detailed motorbike or scooter, side view, filling the lower half; painted city or road background heavily blurred and scrolling; the angry dad driving with the scared kid behind (or two friends). The joke is in the faces and the top label; the shot is a locked side-tracking shot with bob and vibration. For travel, family-on-the-road and friend jokes.
2. **Toon over a Real Road.** The same, but the background is a soft, motion-blurred real-looking road at golden hour (painted to look photographic: tree silhouettes, sky gradient, road with a line, haze). The characters' contrast carries it: one deadpan with half-lidded eyes, the other terrified with huge teeth. A white text box appears mid-reel with the thought. May end on a hard cut to black. For 'my friend vs me' and fear/thrill jokes.
3. **Cel-lit Desi Home with a Style-Switch Cutaway.** A shaded room (purple-dusk or warm walls, diagonal window light, curtain, lamp, patterned armchair); dad in vest and lungi reading a Hindi newspaper, son standing with hands in pockets. Slow push-ins and a few cuts between the two. The punchline is a CUTAWAY in a completely different, richer illustrated style (a retro film-poster street, a vintage car, dad as a 1970s hero in a leather jacket), then back to the plain room. For dad/mom 'in their prime', 'what they say vs what they did'.
4. **Overtake Comparison.** First label: 'girls with their best friend' over a calm scooter ride; the label switches to the 'me with my friend' version and a second vehicle with the chaotic duo drives into the frame and overtakes, while the first pair reacts in the background. Same camera, same blurred street, two vehicles in one frame. For girls vs boys, then vs now, expectation vs reality.
5. **Big-Head Stick Body in a Real Room.** Thin black noodle limbs and a small bean torso, but a BIG, fully shaded head (soft gradient skin, rosy glossy eyes, stubble, wrinkles, teeth) placed in a semi-realistic room (perspective walls, a wooden door with a knob and depth, carpet texture, a padded sofa with cushions and wooden legs). Shot-reverse-shot between two rooms, then an absurd consequence cut (the house reduced to a ruined wall in a golden glow). For 'small act, huge consequence' and family absurdity.

**AUTO RICKSHAW SPEC (draw it so it reads instantly):** three wheels (one small front wheel under the nose, two rear wheels; in side view you see the front wheel and one rear wheel, the rear one slightly bigger); a rounded front nose/cowl with one round headlight and a small indicator each side; a curved front windscreen with a wiper and a black rubber edge; a black or dark-green soft canvas hood (roof) that curves from the windscreen top back over the passenger bench and down at the rear; open sides with a thin vertical pillar and grab rail; the body in two colours, yellow upper and green lower (CNG style) or black lower and yellow upper; a handlebar with mirror; the driver's narrow seat in front and the wide rear passenger bench behind with a back cushion; a spare wheel or a small rear box behind; a number plate with no readable text; a small dashboard. Cel-shade the body panels and add chrome highlights. Draw the driver sitting INSIDE (the hood and pillars in front of and behind him), not on top.

**How to build these in code:** keep using canvas and `render.mjs`, but in a new helper module `studio/src/toon/` (do not use the wobbly Stickman primitives for these looks):
- Smooth paths with `bezierCurveTo`/`quadraticCurveTo` and fixed shapes (no jitter); `lineJoin='round'`.
- `cel(baseShape, base, shadowShape, shade, highlightShape)`: fill base, then `save/clip` to the base and fill the shadow and highlight, then stroke the outline once on top.
- Backgrounds: paint the street or room once into an offscreen canvas, then draw it with `ctx.filter='blur(14px)'` (Chromium canvas supports it) and scroll or pan it; for motion blur draw it 6-8 times with small x offsets at low alpha. Add a sky gradient and a light haze gradient.
- Skin and cloth gradients (`createLinearGradient`) for the Big-Head look; soft drop shadows under people and vehicles (ellipse with alpha).
- Faces: glossy white eyes with a pupil and a small white highlight, eyelids that can droop, brows with thickness, teeth rows, moustache shapes.
- Render speed: offscreen-cache static layers; full 16 s renders should stay under about 5 minutes.
- Before the full render, compare one frame next to these references in your head: if a viewer could not name the vehicle or place in one second, keep detailing.

### VIRAL REEL STYLES (from the owner's 4 reference reels, 2026-11-06; used for YouTube Shorts AND Instagram Reels)
Take the GRAMMAR only: never copy their characters, art, music or jokes (their accounts are stickmyst, uruto_ons, theaifilmmaker and toons_cart). Draw everything in code (canvas, the `studio/src/stickman-gary/engine.js` approach; add a helper module such as `studio/src/reelstyles/` for richer shading, rooms and big-eye faces) and render with the same `render.mjs` pipeline. We do not use AI-generated images.

**Shared grammar of all four:** 9:16; ONE clear set and a character (or two) filling much of the frame; a SETUP LINE of text at the top of the first frame (the relatable 'me when / POV / when you realise' sentence, Hinglish when it fits); 5-8 s for a single beat, up to about 17 s for a dialogue; 0-1 cuts in the original references, with motion inside the frame (blinks, eye rolls, slumps, tears, a prop falling) and one visual payoff or reveal (the Instagram skill now asks for a new shot every 1.5-3 s, so cut more when the channel skill says so); clean outlines with soft shading and a real-feeling background; abrupt ending or a loop. Word captions are optional when the setup line carries the joke; with spoken dialogue add small word captions in the lower third for sound-off viewers. The look rotates per video (VARY EVERY VIDEO).

1. **Moody Mono** (reference: stickmyst). Black-and-white soft-graphite stick figure with a big round head and big eyes, heavy vignette, the only light coming from an object (a phone lock screen, a lamp). Two shots: an object close-up (a huge hand holding the phone, a blank notification screen) then the lonely character lying in bed or sitting in the dark, eyes drifting and rolling. Serif setup line in white at the top, two short lines. Soft melancholic piano or quiet song bed, no jokes spoken. 5-8 s. For loneliness, ghosting, 'nobody noticed', 3 a.m. thoughts, feeling invisible. Night and dark are allowed here because the mood is the point; keep it elegant, not gloomy for its own sake.
2. **Pastel Call** (reference: uruto_ons). Flat cream or pastel background, cute chibi characters with a big head, short bob or ponytail hair and a simple dress or tee, a phone held to the ear. A phone-call dialogue: the first character talks, then a swap to the second character (different hair colour and outfit) in the same frame position, mouths flapping to the voice, small blinks, eye shifts and a head tilt on each beat. Setup line in a small black rounded label with an emoji at the top. 12-18 s. For mental health jokes, gossip, 'friend on the phone', relationship talks. ### Stickman (hand-drawn stick-figure comedy, our own take on a famous Shorts style)
Built from the owner's reference screenshots (a shower-scene short): see `studio/refs/stickman/ref1.png`, `ref2.png`, `ref3.png` in the repo and LOOK at them before building. Inspiration only: never copy a specific channel's characters, scenes or jokes. Best for escalating gags, 'what if' scenarios, everyday-life gags, absurd escalations and two-friend banter skits. This is the proven look: the owner approved the speed and changing frames of the Gary/doors, tadpole and `ep-382.js` videos.

**Style grammar:**
- **Background:** flat desaturated grey-green (sage) with subtle paper/film grain for indoor gags; for banter and anything else prefer a bright minimal OUTDOOR scene (pale sky, sun, two trees, grass, dirt path). Vary the tint per video but keep it pleasant.
- **Room / set:** drawn in the same wobbly thin black line as the characters; simple perspective floor tiles, one wall line, and one big hero prop that is the joke (shower, fridge, rocket, bed, toilet, elevator). Outlined props with flat fills in few colours. Minimal clutter, nothing realistic, no background text.
- **Characters:** thick uniform black noodle lines (about 6-8 px at 1080 wide) with a slight hand-wobble (redraw at 8-12 fps 'boil'). Large round or odd-shaped head with tiny dot or bean eyes, a bumpy nose, simple mouth; small oval or bean torso; thin noodle arms and legs; round mitten hands; oval shoes. Bodies white or light flat colour; no shading. Skin tone can be a flat colour on the head; specs and simple black hair allowed.
- **Expressions:** big and exaggerated: bulging eyes with tiny pupils, red veins, huge screaming mouth with tongue, sweat drops, shaking lines, puffed cheeks. Eyelids for disappointed or sad beats. Lip-sync the mouth to the voice envelope. For two-friend banter, frame BOTH characters in every shot and let the expressions carry the reaction.
- **Motion:** squash and stretch, quick snap poses, wobble and shake on impact, head tilts, bodies flopping. Hand-drawn particle squiggles (water streaks, foam clouds, steam curls, stink lines, impact stars). Strong colour only for the gag, everything else muted. Let each gag play out and settle for a beat before the next.
- **Camera:** 9:16, gentle pushes and small pans (zoom about 1.1-1.4 for two characters), punch-ins on single-character reactions, quick shake on hits; occasional pull-back reveal for the big visual gag (a proven viral move). Scene and shot changes follow the channel's pacing rules.
- **Captions:** big bold sans (Inter 900 or similar), white with a thick black outline, one to three words at a time, lower-middle, popping on each word; key word in a bright colour.
- **Sound:** lively friendly delivery; a light bouncy bed; whoosh/boing/pop/splash/thud on gags only when the channel allows SFX.
- **Make it ours:** each video gets a new hero prop, a new cast (CHARACTER CREATIVITY), a new background tint and a signature recurring gag (a tiny sidekick's reaction; NOT an on-screen counter).

**Build:** the engine is saved in the repo at `studio/src/stickman/` (`index.html` canvas engine with `draw(t)`, `scene.js`, `render.mjs`, `mkaudio8.py`). The newer engine `studio/src/stickman-gary` (`engine.js` helpers; `ep-doors.js`, `ep-382.js` and the Instagram episodes such as `ep-gf2.js` and `ep-scooter2.js` are examples written as scenes `[start, end, fn(t)]` or a single `draw(t)` with camera calls; audio assembler `studio/episodes/2026-11-06-382-fast/asm.py`). Copy it, change the scene, characters, captions and camera, keep improving it. Episode API pattern: `DUR`, `init(ctx,words)` (build line start/end from `words[].line`), `draw(t)`, `cues`. For episodes with a timing split read `data/meta.json` (dur, tb, brake). Narration and credit rules are unchanged.

## SHOT VOCABULARY AND CAMERA (tools; how many shots is decided by each channel)
- (1) wide establishing two-shot with both characters and the setting; (2) medium or close-up of whoever is speaking; (3) REACTION close-ups of the listener (eyes and mouth tell the joke); (4) a cutaway or insert gag (a prop, a phone screen, a thought bubble, the 'trigger' walking in, a neighbour or friend reacting); (5) a fast punch-in or whip-zoom on the reveal; (6) a pull-back reveal; (7) a change of location or angle if the story allows (bench, road, shop); (8) a macro insert into a body part or prop, then a giant overacted face; (9) a phone-screen insert with typing dots as the suspense beat; (10) an object cutaway standing in for a person; (11) a locked side-tracking ride shot with a blurred scrolling background (Polished Desi Toon); (12) a style-switch cutaway into a richer illustrated look for a flashback or fantasy, then back; (13) an overtake: a second vehicle or character enters the same frame and passes.
- Build many scenes `[start, end, fn(t)]`, each with its own camera call `cam(zoom, x, y, shake, t, fn)`, so cuts follow voice timing from `data/words.json`. Cut on each spoken line, on each emotional change and on the trigger moment. A whip pan between scenes can run at the timing boundary +/- 0.1 s.
- Acting size: huge eyes, tiny pupils, open jaws, pop-eyes, sweat and tear marks, slow push-in on a frozen face. The face is the punchline; the line only points at it.
- QA: the contact sheet must show visibly different frames across the video; if two neighbouring seconds look identical, add a cut or a camera move.

- **Both characters in frame.** In two-character scenes keep BOTH in the establishing and payoff shots; use gentle pushes, small pans and angle changes. Close-ups of one character are for a one-character piece or a deliberate reaction cut.
- **No extra on-screen info.** No text in the background, no signs, no handles, no 'tag your friend' cards, no thought clouds, no icons on bodies, no boards that repeat the dialogue, no counters or progress bars. Subtitles only, plus the one top setup line of the viral reel styles. (Small realistic prop text such as a newspaper masthead or a banknote value is allowed when the prop needs it.)
- **Audio: voices plus a light BGM only.** No sound effects unless the owner asks. Say punchline phrases quickly and together without a pause inside them; subtitle commas show where the rhythm splits.
- **Characters:** normal natural hair (black unless the owner says otherwise) unless CHARACTER CREATIVITY is deliberately varied; no old-man looks for young characters; reaction beats understated (disappointed, deadpan) rather than violent unless the owner asks.
- **Abrupt endings** are a funny trend: end right after the punchline reaction, no fade-out, no outro card.
- **Captions:** word-chunk captions timed to the voice, one accent colour on the key word, never covering the characters' faces.

## AUDIO-DRIVEN BUILD (all styles)
1. Generate each voice line once. Trim edges with ffmpeg `silenceremove`, compress only long inner pauses (`stop_periods=-1:stop_duration=0.22` or the per-line CAP method in `asm.py`), optional mild `atempo` to the speed the channel chose.
2. A builder script (`asm.py`, or `studio/src/stickman/mkaudio8.py`) places the lines on a timeline with gaps chosen per the channel brief, writes `timing.json`, word timing for captions (`words.json`), a lip-sync envelope per speaker where used, `meta.json` where an episode needs it, and the mix (`mix.wav`: voices plus a light bed; add SFX only when the channel allows). A voice-only mix (no BGM) is possible.
3. The scene reads that timing: camera cuts at line starts, reactions on exact times, captions chunked across each line proportionally to word length.
4. QA pitfall: envelope keys are named by line prefix; confirm the lip-sync lands on the SPEAKING character (check a frame mid-line).
5. For faceless-shorts engines without Whisper alignment, write `data/lines.json` and `data/words.json` from the timing (words spread by length) and copy the mix to `data/mix.wav`; the variants in `studio/variants/` show how.
